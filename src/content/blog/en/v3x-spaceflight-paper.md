A desktop detector and a satellite payload have very different lives. On a desk, air carries heat away from the electronics. In vacuum there is no air, so heat has to be conducted out through the structure, and the firmware has to keep running for months with nobody around to press reset.

The new paper, *The CosmicWatch Desktop Muon Detector (v3X): Prototype Design and Adaptation for Spaceflight*, by Anvay Ajmera and Spencer Axani, covers both problems: the thermal design of a v3X payload prototype and the changes to the firmware for running unattended.

Read it on arXiv: [2610.07199](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.07199).
