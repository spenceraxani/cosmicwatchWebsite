If a check fails while you build, the cause is nearly always a missing or poor solder joint. Reflow the joints in that part of the board before anything else. These are the common cases from *The Troubleshooting Document*.

:::danger[Smoke from the MAX5026 boost converter]
Unplug immediately. The part is almost certainly on backwards. Remove it and check that pin 1 lines up with pad 1.
:::

## No +30 V at the SiPM connector

The problem is in the DC-DC booster area.

- Check the orientation of the MAX5026.
- Check for more than 3 V across C2. That is the supply from the Pico.
- R1 and R2 set the high-voltage output. Make sure both are soldered.

## +30 V is there, but no pulses at the BNC

Set the oscilloscope to 10 mV/div and 200 ns/div, trigger at 10 mV on the right channel, in Normal mode. You should see pulses of about 10 mV and 100 ns a few times a second.

If you still see nothing, the problem is on the SiPM board:

- **Light leaks.** Lots of noise on the scope means light is getting in. Add tape and check the corners.
- **SiPM orientation or joints.** Check for about 0.5 V across the anode and cathode in diode mode.

## Pulses at the BNC, but the detector does not trigger

Trigger the scope on the BNC and probe the test points with a second channel:

- **TP1** should show about 50× the BNC pulse, **TP3** about 250×. If not, the problem is in that amplifier stage.
- **TP4**, the high-gain peak detector, should show pulses about 250× larger than the BNC that decay over hundreds of microseconds. **TP2**, the low-gain peak detector, about 50×.
- If those look right, check that you loaded the correct firmware.

## Power draw above 0.7 W

Something is shorted, misplaced or backwards. Unplug and go over the board again. The most common mistake is the op amp mounted backwards. Gently touch around the board: nothing should be hot, and a hot part usually points to the problem.
