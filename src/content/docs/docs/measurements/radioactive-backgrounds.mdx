**Shield a detector with lead and see what is left.**

## What you need

- One detector
- Lead bricks or another dense shield
- A day of data with and without the shield

## How it was done

Much of what a single detector counts at sea level is natural radioactivity. You can find sources around the house: old orange Fiestaware plates (glazed with uranium), salt substitute (potassium chloride) and some granite countertops.

To show the effect of shielding, six lead bricks were stacked around one detector for a full day, then the detector logged for another day on a bench away from the lead. The shield removed many of the low pulse-height events, which are mostly background.

Try other materials of the same thickness, or water in a sealed container, and compare how well each one shields.
