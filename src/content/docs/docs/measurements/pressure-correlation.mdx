**Higher pressure means more air overhead and fewer muons.**

## What you need

- Several detector pairs, or a long run with one pair
- Pressure data (the v3X logs its own)

## How it was done

When air pressure goes up there is more atmosphere above you, and fewer muons survive to the ground. The relation is usually written as ΔI / I = β ΔP, where β is the barometric coefficient.

Five coincidence pairs logged for 24.8 days in Madison, Wisconsin, in late 2018. The fit gave β = −0.141 ± 0.007 % per hPa, in line with published values. The v3X has a pressure sensor built in, so you no longer need outside weather data.

One detector pair works too, it just takes longer. A meteorology student has taken detectors into hurricanes, where pressure can drop by over 100 hPa.
